The Exposure on Your Roofs

Every unprotected vent stack is a documented liability.

Multi-family roofs have one to three unprotected vent stack penetrations per unit. Over time, the rubber boots that seal these openings crack, gap, and fail. When they do, wildlife gets in. Water follows. Tenants complain.

The problem is not the wildlife. The problem is what happens when your property files show no documented steps taken against a known entry point. That is when a maintenance complaint becomes a legal exposure.

Real Cost of Inaction

A single wildlife intrusion event with tenant involvement and water damage typically runs $2,000 to $8,000 in remediation costs, before legal fees or claims. For a 100-unit property, closing every entry point costs a fraction of one incident.

Wildlife entry and tenant complaints

Squirrels, birds, and rodents enter through failed vent boots. Tenants hear them. Complaints follow. You are responsible for the remediation and the documentation showing you acted.

Water intrusion through cracked seals

Failed rubber boots allow water into the roof cavity during Florida rain events. By the time it appears on a ceiling, the damage is already significant. Mold remediation is expensive and disruptive.

No documentation of preventive action

If a tenant or HOA board asks what steps were taken to prevent pest intrusion, the answer needs to be documented. "We were not aware" is the worst possible answer in a liability conversation.

Recurring vendor calls and no resolution

Traditional pest control treats symptoms, not entry points. You pay for return visits indefinitely without ever closing the source of the problem. We close the source, permanently.
